## 双语文章 · Is Your Phone Stealing Your Time? / 你的手机在偷走你的时间吗?

> 读法: 先读英文段落,遇到**加粗**的今日新词停一下回想意思,再看下面的中文。

In the 21st **century**, **personal** **technology** has become a **common** part of daily life. Almost **anyone** on a college campus carries a smartphone, and most students **share** their lives online. **However**, a growing body of **evidence** suggests that this new **culture** may come at a **serious** price: our **attention**.

在 21 **世纪**,**个人****科技**产品已经成为日常生活中**普遍**的一部分。大学校园里几乎**任何人**都带着智能手机,大多数学生都在网上**分享**自己的生活。然而,越来越多的**证据**表明,这种新**文化**可能要付出**严重**的代价:我们的**注意力**。

Consider the case of Wang Lin, a second-year student. Last **term**, she noticed she could not **focus** on her books for more than ten minutes. "I would **realize** an hour had passed, and I had done nothing but check messages," she said. Her grades began to **drop**, and the **pressure** made her feel tired and anxious. She **wondered** what had gone wrong.

以二年级学生王琳为例。上**学期**,她发现自己无法**专注**看书超过十分钟。"我会**意识到**一个小时过去了,而我除了查看消息什么也没做,"她说。她的成绩开始**下降**,这种**压力**让她感到疲惫和焦虑。她很**想知道**问题出在哪里。

Wang is not alone. A recent **series** of studies by a health **agency** found that the **amount** of time young people spend on screens has **increased** sharply over the past **decade**. Researchers **identify** several **factors** behind this **rise**. Apps are designed to **seek** our attention: **colorful** alerts, endless **lists** of short videos, and a constant stream of new content. Some experts **argue** that social media works much like a slot machine—each **response** gives us a small **chance** of reward, so we keep checking.

王琳并不是个例。某健康**机构**最近的一**系列**研究发现,过去十**年**里年轻人看屏幕的时**长**急剧**增加**。研究人员**找出**了这种**上升**背后的几个**因素**。应用程序被设计来**寻求**我们的注意力:**五颜六色**的提醒、没完没了的短视频**列表**、源源不断的新内容。一些专家**认为**,社交媒体的工作方式很像老虎机,每一次**回应**都给我们一点得到奖励的**机会**,所以我们不停地查看。

The **effect** is not only on study. Doctors have **recently** linked heavy phone use to poor sleep, eye problems and a lack of **physical** exercise. Sitting still for hours can affect **blood** flow, and in the long run it may raise the **risk** of **disease**. **Medical** **staff** at the university clinic say they see more students with neck pain than ever before. "Your **nature** as a human is to move," one **officer** at the clinic said. "Your body is not built to **lay** still in bed with a phone all day."

影响不只是在学习上。医生们**最近**把过度使用手机与睡眠不好、眼睛问题和缺乏**身体**锻炼联系在一起。一连几个小时坐着不动会影响**血液**流动,长此以往可能增加患**病**的**风险**。大学诊所的**医护人员**说,他们看到脖子疼的学生比以往任何时候都多。"人的**天性**就是要活动,"诊所的一位**工作人员**(**officer**)说。"你的身体不是为了整天拿着手机**躺**在床上而生的。"

So what can students do? The **answer** is not to throw phones away. Phones are useful **tools**, and they are an important **source** of information. The key is to **choose** wisely. Wang decided to take a new **approach**. First, she made a **list** of her **goals** for the **term**. Then she turned off notifications during study **periods** and put her phone in another room at night. She also joined a running club, which gave her more **energy** and a **feeling** of **success**.

那么学生们能做什么呢?答案不是把手机扔掉。手机是有用的工具,也是重要的信息**来源**。关键在于明智地**选择**。王琳决定采取一种新**方法**。她先给这个**学期**列了一份**目标****清单**。然后她在学习**时段**关掉通知,晚上把手机放到另一个房间。她还参加了一个跑步俱乐部,这给了她更多**精力**和一种**成功**的**感觉**。

The change did not happen overnight. "The first week was hard," she admitted. "I kept reaching for my phone **whatever** I was doing." But **step** by **step**, her **ability** to concentrate came back. By the end of the month, she had finished two books and her test scores had gone up. Her **performance** in class was **exactly** what she had hoped for.

改变并非一夜之间发生。"第一周很难,"她承认。"**无论**我在做什么,都会不停地伸手去拿手机。"但一**步**一**步**地,她的专注**能力**回来了。到月底,她读完了两本书,考试成绩也提高了。她在课堂上的**表现**正**是**她所希望的。

Universities are also starting to **deal** with the problem. Some schools now offer **training** courses on "digital health." The **board** of one university even created a special **fund** to help student **organizations** run phone-free events. A student **movement** called "Look Up" has spread across the **region**; its members **represent** dozens of campuses. They **indicate** that the **movement** is not **against** technology; instead, it encourages a healthier **relationship** with it.

各所大学也开始**应对**这个问题。一些学校现在开设"数字健康"**培训**课程。一所大学的**董事会**甚至设立了专项**基金**,帮助学生**组织**举办无手机活动。一场名为"抬起头"的学生**运动**已经在整个**地区**传开,其成员**代表**了几十所高校。他们**表示**,这场**运动**并不反对科技,而是鼓励与科技建立更健康的关系。

Of course, the **issue** is not simple. Companies that make apps earn **billions** of dollars from our **attention**, and **stock** prices often depend on how much time users spend online. Some people say we should **protect** young users with laws, just as we protect them from other dangers. The topic has even entered **political** debate: during the last **election** **campaign** in the United States, both **Republican** and Democratic leaders in **Congress** talked about it, and the **administration** promised to **reduce** the harm of social media. **Thus** the question has moved **beyond** the classroom and into the center of public life.

当然,这个问题并不简单。开发应用的公司从我们的注意力中赚取数十**亿**美元,公司的**股**价往往取决于用户在网上花多少时间。有人说我们应该用法律来**保护**年轻用户,就像保护他们免受其他危险一样。这个话题甚至进入了政治辩论:在美国上一次**选举****竞选**期间,**国会**中的**共和党**和民主党领导人都谈到了它,**政府**也承诺要**减少**社交媒体的危害。**因此**,这个问题已经超出(**beyond**)了课堂,进入了公共生活的**中心**(**central**)。
